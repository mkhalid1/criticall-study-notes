# Data entry and keyboarding

**In one line:** you type names, numbers and addresses you read or hear into the right boxes on screen, fast and without mistakes.

CritiCall has two close cousins here. **Computerized Data Entry** gives you information by voice or in writing and you enter it into a form. **Keyboarding** gives you written paragraphs to copy, and the score is in words per minute. Agencies choose which of these to include.

## What it measures

- **Accuracy.** One wrong digit in a phone number or license plate sends help to the wrong person. Data entry scores speed and accuracy together.
- **Speed.** Keyboarding is scored as words per minute.
- **Putting data in the right place.** The publisher says data must be entered in the proper location on the screen. A perfect phone number typed into the Address box still costs you.
- **Basic computer handling.** Moving between fields, using Tab, and clicking accurately with one left click.

## How the questions look

A blank form appears with fields for caller details. The candidate preparation guide lists the fields in their Tab order:

| Order | Field |
|---|---|
| 1 | Last Name (the cursor starts here on every new form) |
| 2 | First Name |
| 3 | Telephone |
| 4 | Address |
| 5 | City |
| 6 | Zip |
| 7 | Vehicle Identification Number |
| 8 | License Plate |
| 9 | Driver's License (Tab stops here and does not move further) |

Tab moves forward one field. Shift and Tab together move back one field. The guide also warns that many parts of the test only accept UPPER case letters, the way dispatch software often does, and that some test takers find it helps to switch Num Lock on before they start.

In the keyboarding module you see a paragraph and type it as quickly and accurately as you can.

## A method that works

1. **Learn the field order before test day.** If you know Zip comes after City and before the vehicle number, you never have to look for the next box.
2. **Use Tab, not the mouse,** to move forward. Clicking costs time and risks landing in the wrong box.
3. **Chunk numbers.** Read or hear "555-0143" as two chunks, 555 and 0143. Chunks are easier to hold and check than seven loose digits.
4. **Check the risky characters.** Letter O and zero, letter I and one, S and 5, B and 8. License plates and vehicle numbers are full of them.
5. **Type, then glance once.** One quick check of each field before you submit. More than one check costs more time than it saves.
6. **For keyboarding, aim for steady, not frantic.** Errors usually count against you, so a smooth pace with few mistakes beats bursts of speed.

## Traps that cost points

- **Transposing digits.** 0143 becomes 0134. This is the most common data entry error.
- **Dropping a letter in a name.** CHRISTENSEN becomes CHRISTENSN. Names are rarely spelled the way they sound.
- **Mixing up first and last name.** Callers often say "Tran, Kevin" or "Kevin Tran". Listen for which is which.
- **Typing into the wrong field** because you lost track of where the cursor is after a Tab.
- **Pressing Tab in the last field** and expecting it to move on. The guide says the cursor stays in Driver's License.
- **Looking at your hands.** If you cannot touch-type, start practicing now. A few weeks of daily practice makes a real difference.

## Timing

There is no published typing speed that every agency requires. Each agency sets its own standard, and some run a separate typing test as well. For example, the City of Colorado Springs says applicants may be required to take a typing test alongside CritiCall. Check your own job posting for the number that applies to you, and if it gives none, practice until your speed is steady and your error rate is close to zero.

## Practice items

These 5 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from CritiCall or any publisher's materials.

**Item 1.** The cursor is in the License Plate field. You press Shift and Tab together three times. Which field is the cursor in now?
A. Zip B. Address C. City D. Telephone

**Item 2.** The cursor is in the Driver's License field. You press Tab once. Where is the cursor?
A. Last Name B. First Name C. Still in Driver's License D. License Plate

For item 3, the source record is shown first. Choose the entry that matches it exactly.

**Item 3.** Source: License Plate 7KDR214. Driver's License D0482195.
A. 7KDR214 / D0482195
B. 7KRD214 / D0482195
C. 7KDR214 / DO482195
D. 7KDR241 / D0482195

**Item 4.** A caller says: "My number is five five five, oh two six oh." Which number did she give?
A. 555-0206 B. 555-2060 C. 555-0260 D. 555-0620

**Item 5.** Compare the source with the entry. Count the character positions where the entry differs from the source.
Source: 4410 W HOLLIS RD, CARVER 94120
Entry: 4401 W HOLLIS RD, CARVER 94210
A. 0 B. 2 C. 3 D. 4

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: C. City.** Going backwards from License Plate: Vehicle Identification Number (1), Zip (2), City (3). *Why not A:* Zip is two steps back, not three.

**Item 2: C. Still in Driver's License.** The guide says Tab does not move the cursor out of the last field. *Why not A:* it does not loop back to the start.

**Item 3: A.** Every part matches. *B* swaps D and R. *C* uses the letter O instead of zero in the driver's license. *D* swaps the last two digits of the plate.

**Item 4: C. 555-0260.** "Oh two six oh" is 0, 2, 6, 0. *Why not A:* 0206 reverses the last two digits.

**Item 5: D. 4.** The house number 4410 became 4401 (two characters wrong) and the zip 94120 became 94210 (two characters wrong). That is four positions in two swaps. *Why not B:* each swap changes two positions, not one.

## Score yourself

| Item | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Answer | C | C | A | C | D |

- **5:** your eye for detail is sharp. Spend your time on raw typing speed.
- **3 to 4:** slow down on the checking step. Most misses here are swapped digits you read past.
- **2 or fewer:** practice copying names, addresses and phone numbers from a list every day for a week, then check every character.

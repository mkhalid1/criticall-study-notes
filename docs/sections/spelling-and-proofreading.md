# Spelling and proofreading

**In one line:** you spell commonly confused words correctly, and you fix spelling, punctuation and grammar errors in a short document.

The **Spelling** test checks commonly misspelled or misused words, and you type your answers. The candidate preparation guide gives the reason: a sound-alike error, such as "patience" for "patients", could delay help that someone badly needs. The **Proofreading** test gives you a document with spelling, punctuation and grammar errors and you edit it directly. The publisher lists four English proofreading tests and one in Spanish, and agencies can build their own.

## What it measures

- **Sound-alike words.** Patients and patience, breath and breathe, their and there.
- **Commonly misspelled words.** Words like occurred, unconscious and ambulance that people get wrong under pressure.
- **Careful reading.** Spotting an error in a sentence that "looks right" at a glance.

## How the questions look

**Spelling.** A sentence with a blank, or a word read aloud, and you type the correct spelling. Some formats show several spellings and ask you to pick the right one.

**Proofreading.** A short memo or report appears with errors in it. You correct the text on screen.

## A method that works

1. **Learn the confusable pairs as pairs.** Know both words and when each is used. A list is at the end of this page.
2. **For a blank, decide the meaning first,** then the spelling. "The ___ were taken to the hospital" needs people (patients), not a quality (patience).
3. **Proofread in two passes.** First pass for meaning and grammar. Second pass word by word for spelling, reading slowly.
4. **Check the usual suspects:** double letters (occurred, accommodate), ie and ei (receive, seizure), silent letters (pneumonia), and endings (-ence or -ance).
5. **Do not "fix" what is not broken.** Changing a correct word costs a point just like missing an error.

## Traps that cost points

- **Spellcheck habit.** You will not have red underlines. Train without them.
- **Correct spelling, wrong word.** "Break" and "brake" are both real words. Only one fits.
- **Reading for sense instead of letters.** Your brain fills in "recieve" as "receive". Slow down on the second pass.
- **Overcorrecting.** Adding commas or changing style when only errors are being marked.

## Timing

Spelling items take seconds if you know the word and much longer if you do not, so the time to prepare is now. Proofreading takes longer: read the whole document once before changing anything.

## Practice items

These 12 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from CritiCall or any publisher's materials.

For items 1 to 6, choose the correct word for the blank.

**Item 1.** Two ___ were taken to County General after the crash.
A. patience B. patients C. patents D. patience's

**Item 2.** The caller said her husband could not ___ properly.
A. breathes B. breth C. breath D. breathe

**Item 3.** The truck's ___ failed on the hill.
A. brakes B. breaks C. brake's D. braiks

**Item 4.** The suspect ran out of ___ behind the warehouse.
A. site B. cite C. sight D. sighte

**Item 5.** The man was found ___ on the kitchen floor.
A. unconscience B. unconscious C. unconcious D. unconsious

**Item 6.** ___ car is still parked in the fire lane.
A. There B. They're C. Their D. Thier

For items 7 to 10, choose the correctly spelled word.

**Item 7.** A. ocurred B. occured C. occurred D. ocurrred

**Item 8.** A. seizure B. siezure C. seisure D. seizeure

**Item 9.** A. ambulanse B. ambulence C. ambulanc D. ambulance

**Item 10.** A. intersectoin B. intresection C. intersection D. intersecion

**Item 11.** Which sentence has no errors?
A. The caller recieved a text from her son at 10:15.
B. The caller received a text from her son at 10:15.
C. The caller received a text from her son, at 10:15
D. The caller recieved a text from her son at 10:15.

**Item 12.** How many errors does this sentence contain? Count each wrong word or missing mark as one error.
"The officer said there car was parked to close to the hydrant"
A. 1 B. 2 C. 3 D. 4

<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 12</summary>

**Item 1: B. patients.** People receiving medical care. *Why not A:* patience is the ability to wait calmly, which is exactly the mix-up the candidate guide warns about.

**Item 2: D. breathe.** The verb. *Why not C:* breath is the noun ("a deep breath").

**Item 3: A. brakes.** The parts that stop a vehicle. *Why not B:* breaks means pieces snapping or pauses.

**Item 4: C. sight.** Out of sight means where he could not be seen. *Why not A:* a site is a place, such as a building site.

**Item 5: B. unconscious.** *Why not C:* it drops the s before the c.

**Item 6: C. Their.** It shows whose car. *Why not A:* there is a place. *Why not B:* they're means they are.

**Item 7: C. occurred.** Double c and double r.

**Item 8: A. seizure.** One of the exceptions to "i before e".

**Item 9: D. ambulance.** Ends in -ance.

**Item 10: C. intersection.**

**Item 11: B.** *A* and *D* misspell received. *C* adds an unneeded comma and drops the period at the end.

**Item 12: C. 3.** "there" should be "their", "to close" should be "too close", and the sentence is missing its period. *Why not B:* the missing period at the end counts as an error too.

## Words worth knowing

These come up again and again in dispatch notes. Learn them in pairs where they have a twin.

| Word | Do not confuse with |
|---|---|
| patients (people being treated) | patience (calm waiting) |
| breathe (verb) | breath (noun) |
| brake (stops a car) | break (snap, pause) |
| sight (seeing) | site (place), cite (quote) |
| their (belonging to them) | there (place), they're (they are) |
| too (also, excessively) | to, two |
| accept (receive) | except (leave out) |
| lose (misplace) | loose (not tight) |
| conscious (awake) | conscience (sense of right) |
| affect (verb, to change) | effect (noun, a result) |
| occurred, unconscious, seizure, ambulance, intersection, received, separate, vehicle, emergency, accident, license, pneumonia | |

# Probability and numerical ability

**In one line:** you pick the most likely answer from conflicting reports, and you do quick, job-style arithmetic with times, distances and percentages.

Two CritiCall tests sit here. **Frequency of Information / Probability Determination** asks you to choose the most likely solution from the information given, apply basic probability, and ignore what does not matter. **Numerical Ability** covers job-related addition, subtraction and percentages in real-life settings such as distances, times and amounts.

## What it measures

- **Weighing reports.** Five callers describe the same car in four different ways. Which description is most likely right?
- **Filtering.** Not every number in a question is needed.
- **Everyday arithmetic.** Response times, distances, counts and simple percentages, without a calculator in your head slowing you down.

## How the questions look

**Probability.** You get a set of reports, such as several witness descriptions, and choose the most likely fact. The usual logic: when independent reports conflict, the detail that comes up most often is the most likely one.

**Numerical.** Short word problems: a unit was dispatched at one time and arrived at another, a route has several legs, a shift handled some percentage of calls of one type.

The publisher does not say whether a calculator is allowed. Practice without one.

## A method that works

1. **Probability: tally first.** List each answer and how many reports gave it. The highest count is usually your answer.
2. **Check what the question asks.** "Most likely color" and "most likely make" use different columns of the same reports.
3. **Numerical: write the operation before the numbers.** "Arrival minus dispatch" or "part divided by whole". It stops you adding when you should subtract.
4. **Times: work in minutes past the hour.** From 14:52 to 15:09 is 8 minutes to 15:00, then 9 more: 17 minutes.
5. **Percentages: use 10%.** 15% of 240 is 10% (24) plus 5% (12), so 36.
6. **Sanity-check the size of the answer.** A response time of 170 minutes or a 300% share means a slip.

## Traps that cost points

- **Picking the most vivid report** instead of the most common one.
- **Crossing the hour wrongly.** 10:55 to 11:10 is 15 minutes, not 55.
- **Using every number given.** Some numbers are there to distract.
- **Percentage of the wrong total.** "30% of the calls were medical, and half of those were falls" means 15% of all calls, not 50%.
- **Speed and time units.** At 30 miles per hour you cover half a mile a minute, not 30.

## Timing

Most items should take 30 to 60 seconds. If an item needs long working, check whether you are using a number you do not need.

## Practice items

These 12 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from CritiCall or any publisher's materials. No calculator.

**Item 1.** Six callers describe a car leaving a robbery: blue, blue, green, blue, black, blue. What is the most likely color?
A. Green B. Black C. Blue D. Cannot tell

**Item 2.** Five witnesses give a suspect's height: 5 ft 10, 6 ft 0, 6 ft 0, 5 ft 8, 6 ft 0. What is the most likely height?
A. 5 ft 8 B. 5 ft 10 C. 5 ft 11 D. 6 ft 0

**Item 3.** Four callers report where a fight is happening: "outside the cinema on Hale Street", "Hale Street near the cinema", "by the bus stop on Hale Street, opposite the cinema", and "on Ross Avenue". Where should the first unit go?
A. Ross Avenue B. Hale Street, by the cinema C. The bus depot D. Cannot tell

**Item 4.** Over the past year, 18 of the 24 calls from a payphone at a bus station were false alarms. A new call comes in from it. Based only on this record, is the call more likely to be a false alarm or a real one?
A. More likely a false alarm B. More likely a real call C. Equally likely D. Cannot tell

**Item 5.** A unit was dispatched at 14:52 and arrived at 15:09. What was the response time?
A. 13 minutes B. 17 minutes C. 21 minutes D. 57 minutes

**Item 6.** A center handled 240 calls on one shift. 15% were medical calls. How many medical calls were there?
A. 24 B. 30 C. 36 D. 48

**Item 7.** An ambulance is 3 miles away and travels at an average of 45 miles per hour. How many minutes will it take to arrive?
A. 3 B. 4 C. 6 D. 15

**Item 8.** A route has three legs: 1.4 miles, 0.8 miles and 2.3 miles. What is the total distance?
A. 4.1 miles B. 5.4 miles C. 4.3 miles D. 4.5 miles

**Item 9.** A center had 80 calls in an hour. 25 were police calls, 30 were medical, and the rest were fire calls. What percentage were fire calls?
A. 25% B. 31.25% C. 30% D. 37.5%

**Item 10.** A crew started a call at 23:40 and cleared it 2 hours 35 minutes later. What time did they clear?
A. 01:15 B. 02:05 C. 02:15 D. 01:75

**Item 11.** Of 200 calls, 40% were medical. Of the medical calls, 25% were falls. How many calls were falls?
A. 80 B. 50 C. 10 D. 20

**Item 12.** Unit 1 is 2.5 miles from a crash and Unit 2 is 4 miles away. Both travel at 30 miles per hour. How many minutes sooner does Unit 1 arrive?
A. 1.5 B. 2 C. 3 D. 5

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 12</summary>

**Item 1: C. Blue.** Four of six callers said blue. *Why not D:* conflicting reports still point to a most likely answer.

**Item 2: D. 6 ft 0.** Three of five witnesses said 6 ft 0. *Why not C:* 5 ft 11 is close to the average, but no one reported it. The question asks for the most likely reported height, so go with the most frequent.

**Item 3: B. Hale Street, by the cinema.** Three of four callers place it on Hale Street near the cinema, including the bus stop opposite. Only one says Ross Avenue. *Why not C:* "bus stop" is not a bus depot.

**Item 4: A. More likely a false alarm.** 18 of 24 is 75%. Based only on the record, a false alarm is more likely. *Why not D:* the question tells you to use the record alone. (A real center would still answer the call as if it were real.)

**Item 5: B. 17 minutes.** 14:52 to 15:00 is 8 minutes, plus 9 minutes to 15:09. *Why not C:* 21 comes from subtracting 52 from 9 the wrong way round.

**Item 6: C. 36.** 10% of 240 is 24, 5% is 12, so 15% is 36.

**Item 7: B. 4 minutes.** 45 miles per hour is 45 miles in 60 minutes, so 3 miles in 4 minutes (60 divided by 45, times 3). *Why not D:* 15 minutes would mean 15 miles per hour.

**Item 8: D. 4.5 miles.** 1.4 plus 0.8 is 2.2, plus 2.3 is 4.5.

**Item 9: B. 31.25%.** Fire calls: 80 minus 25 minus 30 is 25. 25 out of 80 is 31.25%. *Why not A:* 25% would be 20 calls. *Why not D:* 37.5% is the medical share (30 out of 80).

**Item 10: C. 02:15.** 23:40 plus 2 hours is 01:40, plus 35 minutes is 02:15. *Why not D:* there is no 75th minute.

**Item 11: D. 20.** 40% of 200 is 80 medical calls. 25% of 80 is 20. *Why not A:* 80 is all the medical calls, not the falls.

**Item 12: C. 3 minutes.** At 30 miles per hour a unit covers 1 mile in 2 minutes. Unit 1 takes 5 minutes, Unit 2 takes 8 minutes. The difference is 3. *Why not A:* 1.5 is the difference in miles, not minutes.

</details>

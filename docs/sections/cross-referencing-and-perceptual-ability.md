# Cross-referencing and perceptual ability

**In one line:** you look things up in a list fast, and you spot whether two strings of letters and numbers are exactly the same.

These are two separate CritiCall tests that use the same skill. **Cross Referencing** checks alphabetic, character recognition and numeric searching. You answer written and spoken requests for information from a list. **Perceptual Ability** checks how well you compare letter and number sequences and rule out ones that look alike but are not.

## What it measures

- **Search speed.** Finding a name in an alphabetical list, or a record by its number.
- **Precision.** Seeing that 4417 and 4471 are different when you only glance at them.
- **Following a request exactly.** Returning the field you were asked for, not the one next to it.

## How the questions look

**Cross Referencing.** A table appears, something like a phone directory or a list of units, and a request comes in by text or by voice: "What is the address for Harlow, Dana?" You find the row and give the right field. The candidate preparation guide describes finding a name in an alphabetical telephone book as one of the skills measured.

**Perceptual Ability.** Two strings sit side by side, such as two addresses or two plate numbers. You decide if they are the same or different, or which one in a group matches a target.

## A method that works

1. **Use the sort order.** In an alphabetical list, jump straight to the right letter, then the second letter. Do not read from the top.
2. **Confirm the full name.** Two people can share a surname. Check the first name too.
3. **Move your eyes along the row with a finger or the cursor.** Rows are where you slip onto the line above or below.
4. **Compare in chunks.** Split 6ZB4417 into 6ZB and 4417. Compare each chunk, left to right.
5. **Hunt for the usual swaps:** reversed pairs (17 and 71), look-alike characters (O and 0, I and 1, S and 5, B and 8), and dropped or doubled letters.

## Traps that cost points

- **Reading the right row, wrong column.** You find the person, then give their phone number when the request was for the address.
- **Stopping at the first surname match** when there are two people with that surname.
- **Seeing what you expect.** Your brain corrects small differences. Slow down on the second half of a string, where most differences hide.
- **Treating "almost the same" as the same.** One character is enough to make two strings different.

## Timing

Each lookup should take only a few seconds once you use the sort order. Comparison items should take two or three seconds each. If you are slower, practice with any printed list, such as a contacts list or a bus timetable.

### Comparisons for items 3 to 5

The strings are shown in a fixed-width font, as they would be on a dispatch screen. For items 3 and 4, decide whether the two strings are **Same** or **Different**.

**Item 3.** `7KDR214` / `7KDR2l4`

**Item 4.** `1HGCM82633A004352` / `1HGCM82633A004352`

**Item 5.** How many of these four pairs are exactly the same?
(1) `555-0912` / `555-0912` (2) `MARQUETTE` / `MARQUETE` (3) `88 ELM CT` / `88 ELM CT` (4) `W2X9P0` / `W2X9PO`
A. 1 B. 2 C. 3 D. 4

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: B. 1150 VESTRY LN.** There are two people named HARLOW. Dennis is the second. *Why not A:* that is Dana's address. *Why not D:* the street type is LN, not RD.

**Item 2: A. 902 SHELDON AVE.** 555-0287 belongs to BOATENG, AMA. *Why not D:* SHELTON is one letter off.

**Item 3: Different.** The second string has a lowercase letter l where the first has the digit 1. *Why it matters:* in a plate search, that is a different vehicle.

**Item 4: Same.** Compare in chunks: 1HGCM, 82633, A0043, 52. All match.

**Item 5: B. 2.** Pairs 1 and 3 match. Pair 2 drops a T from MARQUETTE. Pair 4 ends with the digit 0 in one string and the letter O in the other.

# Call summarization

**In one line:** you listen to a caller describe an incident, then type or choose the facts a responder needs, and leave out the rest.

The publisher calls this Oral Comprehension, in two levels. **Call Summarization 1** tests general note taking from spoken information. **Call Summarization 2** is a harder version that wants more detailed answers. Other variants add emergency-message decisions on top while you listen (the "MT" and "E911" versions in the publisher's test list).

## What it measures

- **Listening accuracy.** Hearing a plate, a street name or a direction correctly the first time.
- **Filtering.** Callers ramble. The publisher says this test checks your ability to filter out non-essential distractions and summarize the important facts.
- **Note taking at the keyboard.** You type notes while the caller is still talking.

## How the questions look

On the real test you hear a recorded call over a headset. You may be able to adjust the volume. Then you answer questions or type a short summary. You cannot replay a real 911 call, so expect to hear each recording once.

A static page cannot play audio, so the practice below uses written transcripts. To keep it honest, **open each transcript, read it once at normal speaking pace (about 30 seconds), close it, then answer** without looking back. If you can, have a friend read the transcript aloud to you instead. That is closer to the real thing.

## A method that works

1. **Know what responders always need** before the call starts: what happened, where, when, who is hurt, who did it, what they look like, which way they went, and how to call the caller back.
2. **Type in keywords, not sentences.** "BLUE PICKUP, PARTIAL PLATE 6RJ, NB GARNET" is faster and clearer than a full sentence.
3. **Write numbers and names the moment you hear them.** These are the facts that fade first.
4. **Skip the chatter.** The caller's opinion of the driver, what they were doing before, the weather: leave it unless it changes the response.
5. **Read your notes once before answering** and fill any gap you remember.

## Traps that cost points

- **Writing down everything.** You fall behind and miss the plate number at the end.
- **Mixing up left and right, north and south.** If a caller says "he went toward the river", note that, and do not turn it into a compass direction you guessed.
- **Trusting the summary that sounds most complete.** In multiple-choice summaries, the longest option often includes a detail the caller never said. Check every fact in it.
- **Losing the caller's own details.** Callback number and location of the caller are easy to forget because they come at the start.
- **Adding assumptions.** If the caller says "I think he was drunk", the fact is that she thinks so, not that he was.

## Timing

Your time is set by the recording. The work is keeping up. Practice by typing notes while listening to a news report or a podcast, then checking what you missed. Ten minutes a day for a week will make your notes faster and shorter.

## Practice items

These 5 items are original, written for these notes, and so are the two calls. They are not taken from CritiCall or any publisher's materials.

### Call 1

<details>
<summary>Call 1 transcript: open, read once, close</summary>

> "Hi, yes, I need to report a hit and run. I'm at the corner of Garnet Avenue and Fourth Street, outside the bakery. A guy on a bike just got hit by a blue pickup truck, one of the big ones, kind of faded paint. The truck didn't even stop, it went north on Garnet. I only got part of the plate, it started with six, R, J. The cyclist is sitting up on the curb, he's awake and talking but his leg is bleeding a lot. Honestly this corner is always a nightmare, I've told the council before. Oh, and my dog is with me, sorry if you hear barking. My name is Paula and my cell is 555-0172."

</details>

**Item 1.** Which way did the vehicle go?
A. South on Garnet Avenue B. North on Fourth Street C. North on Garnet Avenue D. East on Fourth Street

**Item 2.** What part of the license plate did the caller see?
A. 6RJ B. R6J C. 6JR D. 8RJ

**Item 3.** Which summary is the most accurate and complete for responders?
A. Hit and run at Garnet Ave and 4th St. Blue pickup, partial plate 6RJ, fled north on Garnet. Cyclist awake, leg bleeding heavily. Caller Paula, 555-0172.
B. Hit and run at Garnet Ave and 4th St. Faded blue pickup driven by a drunk driver, plate 6RJ, fled north. Cyclist unconscious. Caller Paula.
C. Cyclist hurt near a bakery. Dangerous corner, complaints made to council. Caller has a dog. Truck left the scene.
D. Hit and run at Garnet Ave and 4th St. Blue pickup fled south on Garnet. Cyclist awake. Caller Paula, 555-0172.

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 3</summary>

**Item 1: C. North on Garnet Avenue.** "It went north on Garnet." *Why not B:* Fourth Street is the cross street where the crash happened, not the direction of travel.

**Item 2: A. 6RJ.** "Started with six, R, J." *Why not B or C:* these change the order, which would send officers looking for the wrong vehicle.

**Item 3: A.** It has the location, vehicle, partial plate, direction, injury and callback, with nothing invented. *B* adds "drunk", which the caller never said, calls the plate complete, and says the cyclist is unconscious. *C* keeps the chatter and loses the facts. *D* has the wrong direction.

</details>

### Call 2

<details>
<summary>Call 2 transcript: open, read once, close</summary>

> "Hi, um, I'm in the parking garage at the Westfield office park, the one on Lowry Road. I'm on level three, sitting in my car. There's a man walking down the row pulling on car door handles, like he's checking which ones are open. He's tall, white, maybe in his twenties, wearing a gray hoodie with the hood up, dark jeans and white sneakers, and he's got a black backpack. He just got one open, a silver hatchback, and he's leaning inside it now. Wait, he's out, he's walking toward the stairwell by the elevators. I don't want him to see me. My number is 555-0349, my name's Deb."

</details>

**Item 4.** What was the man wearing on his feet?
A. Black boots B. White sneakers C. Gray sneakers D. The caller did not say

**Item 5.** Which summary is the most accurate and complete?
A. Suspicious man in Westfield garage, Lowry Rd. Gray hoodie, white sneakers. Stole a silver hatchback.
B. Westfield office park garage, Lowry Rd, level 3. Man trying car door handles, entered silver hatchback. Tall, white, 20s, gray hoodie up, dark jeans, white sneakers, black backpack. Now heading to stairwell by elevators. Caller Deb in her car, 555-0349.
C. Westfield garage level 3. Tall man in gray hoodie breaking car windows, armed, heading to stairwell. Caller Deb, 555-0349.
D. Westfield office park garage, level 3. Man trying door handles. Tall, 20s, black hoodie, black backpack. Heading to exit ramp. Caller Deb, 555-0349.

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 4 and 5</summary>

**Item 4: B. White sneakers.** *Why not C:* gray was the hoodie, not the shoes. Colors drift between items when you do not note them as you hear them.

**Item 5: B.** It is the only option with the location, level, behavior, full description, direction and callback, and nothing invented. *A* says the car was stolen. *C* adds broken windows and a weapon. *D* has the wrong hoodie color and the wrong direction.

</details>

## Score yourself

| Item | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Answer | C | A | A | B | B |

- **5:** strong listening. Practice again with someone reading faster.
- **3 to 4:** check which kind of fact you lost. Numbers and directions are the usual ones. Note those first.
- **2 or fewer:** before the next attempt, write the list "what, where, who, description, direction, callback" at the top of your notes and fill it as you read.

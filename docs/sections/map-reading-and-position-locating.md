# Map reading and position locating

**In one line:** you read a very simple map to pick the most direct legal route, or to work out where a caller is from the clues they give you.

These are two CritiCall tests. **Map Reading / Geographic Directions** asks you to choose the most direct route to a destination while obeying every regulatory sign. **Position Locating** asks you to find a caller's position on a map from spoken or written clues, which means piecing separate facts together.

## What it measures

- **Route finding.** Seeing the shortest path on a grid.
- **Rule compliance.** One-way streets and closures are not suggestions. The publisher says route choices must comply with all regulatory signs.
- **Direction sense.** Knowing that a left turn while heading south points you east.
- **Combining clues.** "Near the water tower, south of the tracks, by the river" only points to one square if you use all three.

## How the questions look

The publisher's applicant FAQ says CritiCall maps hold only very basic features: streets, intersections, bridges, rivers and railroad tracks. There are no contour lines or detailed features. No map-reading training is needed, but you must be comfortable with left and right and with north, south, east and west.

## A method that works

1. **Find north first.** Every map has a compass. Fix which way is up before you read the question.
2. **Mark the obstacles.** Before tracing any route, spot every one-way arrow, closed road, river and bridge. These decide most questions.
3. **Count blocks.** On a grid with equal blocks, the shortest route is the one with the fewest blocks. Count each option instead of eyeballing it.
4. **Check each leg of a route against the signs.** A route is only as legal as its worst block.
5. **For turns, picture yourself in the car.** Heading south, your left is east. Heading west, your right is north. If it helps, turn the page.
6. **For position clues, use them one at a time** to cross out squares, then check the answer against every clue.

## Traps that cost points

- **Taking the shortest route that breaks a rule.** It is not the most direct legal route, so it is wrong.
- **Forgetting that a river needs a bridge.** A straight line across water is not a road.
- **Going the wrong way down a one-way street** because it is the obvious shortcut.
- **Flipping left and right** when heading south or west.
- **Mixing up rows and columns** in grid references. B2 means column B, row 2.
- **Stopping at the first clue** in a position question. The other clues are there to rule out the near misses.

## Timing

Most map items take 20 to 40 seconds once you have a routine. The slow part is the first look at a new map, so spend ten seconds studying its signs and bridges before the first question.

## Practice items

These 5 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from CritiCall or any publisher's materials. Use Map A for items 1 to 3.

**Item 1.** Which is the most direct legal route from Fire Station 1 to City Hall?
A. East on Oak St all the way to 4th Ave.
B. South on 1st Ave to Pine St, east on Pine St to 4th Ave, north on 4th Ave to Oak St.
C. South on 1st Ave to Elm St, east on Elm St to 4th Ave, north on 4th Ave to Oak St.
D. East on Oak St to 2nd Ave, south on 2nd Ave to Cedar St, east on Cedar St to 4th Ave, north on 4th Ave to Oak St.

**Item 2.** Which is the most direct legal route from City Hall to the Library?
A. West on Oak St to 3rd Ave, south on 3rd Ave to Pine St.
B. South on 4th Ave to Pine St, west on Pine St to 3rd Ave.
C. South on 4th Ave to Elm St, west on Elm St to 3rd Ave, north on 3rd Ave to Pine St.
D. South on 4th Ave to Elm St, west on Elm St to 2nd Ave, north on 2nd Ave to Pine St, east on Pine St to 3rd Ave.

**Item 3.** You are driving east on Elm St and turn right onto 2nd Ave. You drive one block. Which street do you reach?
A. Pine St B. Oak St C. Cedar St D. 3rd Ave

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 3</summary>

**Item 1: C.** It is legal and 7 blocks long (2 south, 3 east, 2 north). *A* uses the closed block of Oak St. *B* goes east on Pine St, which is one way westbound. *D* is legal but 9 blocks long, because it crosses the river and comes back. Since Oak St is closed east of 3rd Ave and Pine St only runs west, every legal route has to reach 4th Ave on Elm St or further south, so 7 blocks is the shortest possible.

**Item 2: B.** Two blocks, and Pine St runs westbound, which is the direction you need. *A* uses the closed block of Oak St. *C* goes north on 3rd Ave, which is one way southbound. *D* goes east on Pine St, against the one-way.

**Item 3: C. Cedar St.** Turning right while heading east points you south. One block south of Elm St on 2nd Ave crosses the bridge to Cedar St. *Why not A:* Pine St is north of Elm St, which would be a left turn.

</details>

Use Map B for items 4 and 5.

**Item 4.** A caller is one square west of the Hospital and just north of the railroad. Which square?
A. E4 B. E3 C. F2 D. D3

**Item 5.** A caller is in the square that touches the Water Tower's square only at its south-east corner. Which square?
A. C3 B. A3 C. C1 D. B3

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 4 and 5</summary>

**Item 4: B. E3.** The Hospital is in F3. One square west is E3, and row 3 is just north of the railroad. *Why not A:* E4 is south of the tracks.

**Item 5: A. C3.** The Water Tower is in B2. South-east diagonally is one column east (C) and one row south (3). *Why not D:* B3 shares a whole side, not just a corner.

</details>

# Reading comprehension and sentence clarity

**In one line:** you read short passages and answer questions about the details, and you pick the clearest way to write a set of facts.

**Reading Comprehension** measures your ability to read, understand and keep hold of details in a short paragraph. **Sentence Clarity** checks whether you can tell which version of a sentence presents a set of facts most clearly. Both matter because dispatchers read policies and incident notes all day, and write notes that someone else must understand in a hurry.

## What it measures

- **Detail retention.** Not just the gist, but the number, the exception and the condition.
- **Reading what is there.** Answering from the passage, not from what seems sensible.
- **Clear writing.** Recognizing a sentence that says one thing, once, in the right order, with no confusing "he" or "it".

## How the questions look

**Reading comprehension.** A short paragraph, often a work policy or an incident description, followed by multiple-choice questions.

**Sentence clarity.** Four versions of the same facts. You choose the clearest. The wrong ones usually have a vague pronoun ("he" could be either man), a modifier in the wrong place ("bleeding, the officer found the dog"), facts in a confusing order, or extra words.

## A method that works

1. **Read the question first** on comprehension items, so you know what detail to look for.
2. **Read the passage once, carefully.** Watch for words that limit a rule: only, unless, except, all, never, within, before.
3. **Find the sentence that answers the question.** Point to it. If you cannot point to it, your answer may come from outside the passage.
4. **For clarity items, ask three questions of each option:** Is every "he", "she" or "it" clear? Is each describing phrase next to the thing it describes? Is every fact there, once?
5. **Prefer short and specific.** When two options are both correct, the clearer one is usually the shorter one.

## Traps that cost points

- **Answering from common sense.** If the policy says "within 10 minutes", the answer is 10, even if 5 feels better.
- **Missing the exception.** "All calls are recorded except internal transfers" makes a question about transfers easy, if you saw the word except.
- **Choosing the longest clarity option** because it seems most complete. Extra words often add confusion.
- **Ignoring pronouns.** "The driver told the officer he was injured" does not say who was hurt.

## Timing

Comprehension passages should take about a minute to read, then about 20 seconds per question. Clarity items should take 15 to 30 seconds each.

## Practice items

These 5 items are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from CritiCall or any publisher's materials.

### Passage 1

> Riverside Communications Center policy on callbacks: When a 911 call disconnects before the caller gives a location, the call taker must call the number back within two minutes. If no one answers, the call taker leaves a voicemail asking the caller to call 911 again, and then asks a supervisor whether to send a unit to the address linked to the phone. Calls from payphones are not called back. Instead, the nearest available unit is sent to the payphone's location. Every callback attempt must be logged, including the time and the result.

**Item 1.** What happens when a payphone call disconnects before the location is given?
A. The call taker calls the payphone back B. A voicemail is left C. The nearest available unit is sent to the payphone D. A supervisor decides

**Item 2.** No one answers the callback. Which step comes next under the policy?
A. Send a unit straight to the address B. Leave a voicemail, then ask a supervisor about sending a unit C. Close the call D. Call back again every two minutes

### Passage 2

> At 06:40 a delivery driver reported a white van parked across both lanes of Mill Road, just past the railway bridge, with its hazard lights on. The driver could not see anyone inside. A second caller at 06:47 said the van's driver was sitting on the verge nearby, holding his arm, and seemed confused. A police unit was sent at 06:42 for the blocked road. An ambulance was added at 06:48.

**Item 3.** How many minutes passed between the first report and the ambulance being added?
A. 6 B. 7 C. 8 D. 2

### Sentence clarity

Choose the clearest version.

**Item 4.**
A. The officer told the driver that he would need to move his car.
B. The officer told the driver to move the driver's car.
C. The officer told the driver, "You need to move your car."
D. The officer told him that he would need to move it.

**Item 5.**
A. Barking loudly, the woman called about her neighbor's dog.
B. The woman called about her neighbor's dog, which was barking loudly.
C. The woman, barking loudly, called about the dog of her neighbor.
D. About her neighbor's dog barking loudly, the woman called.

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: C.** Payphone calls are not called back. The nearest available unit is sent instead. *Why not A:* the policy says payphones are not called back.

**Item 2: B.** If no one answers, leave a voicemail, then ask a supervisor whether to send a unit. *Why not A:* sending a unit needs the supervisor step first.

**Item 3: C. 8 minutes.** First report at 06:40, ambulance added at 06:48. *Why not B:* 7 minutes is the gap to the second call, not to the ambulance. *Why not D:* 2 minutes is the gap to the police unit.

**Item 4: C.** The quoted words make it clear who must move whose car. *A* and *D* leave "he" unclear. *B* is clear but clumsy.

**Item 5: B.** The barking belongs to the dog, and "which was barking loudly" sits right next to it. *A* and *C* make the woman bark.

## Score yourself

| Answer | C | B | C | C | B |

- **5:** strong reader and writer. Light review only.
- **3 to 4:** for comprehension misses, check whether you skipped a limiting word. For clarity misses, check pronouns and phrase placement.
- **2 or fewer:** read one short news story a day and write a three-line summary a stranger could follow.

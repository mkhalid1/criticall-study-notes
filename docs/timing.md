# Timing and pacing

CritiCall does not have one clock. Each module has its own timing, some items are on a hard per-item limit, and the whole session can run for hours. Pacing here means managing three things: the seconds on a single item, the minutes in a module, and your energy across the session.

## The budget

| Level | What the publisher says | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Whole session | One to three hours on average, depending on the modules your agency picks. The help center suggests setting aside two to three hours to finish in one sitting. | Book a quiet block of at least three hours. Do not squeeze it into a lunch break. |
| Emergency messages | 15 seconds per message, and no credit if you are late | Know the four rules so well that each decision takes about five seconds |
| Audio items | You usually hear each one once | Type numbers and names the moment you hear them |
| Practice items | Optional, and not scored | Do them. They are free time to learn each screen. |

Sources: [CritiCall applicant FAQ](https://criticall911.com/company/news/criticall-dispatcher-applicant), [TestGenius help center](https://applicant.testgenius.com/knowledgebase/how-long-does-the-test-take) and the [candidate preparation guide](https://criticall911.com/hubfs/Criticall-Website-Files/CritiCall_Candidate_Test_Preparation_Guide_2023.pdf).

## Item-level pacing

These are our study targets, not publisher rules. They are what we would aim for in practice so there is a margin on the day.

| Item type | Target in practice |
|---|---|
| Emergency-message decision | 5 seconds or less |
| Same-or-different comparison | 2 to 3 seconds |
| Directory lookup | 5 to 10 seconds |
| Map route question | 20 to 40 seconds |
| Numerical word problem | 30 to 60 seconds |
| Situational judgment item | 30 to 45 seconds |
| Reading passage | About 1 minute to read, then 20 seconds per question |

## Where time usually goes

- **Rereading.** Reading a message or a map twice is the most common time sink. Train to read once, carefully.
- **Second-guessing memory items.** Your first recall is usually your best.
- **The mouse.** Reaching for the mouse to move between fields when Tab would do. Practice moving through forms with the keyboard.
- **Recovering from an interruption.** When an emergency message breaks into a task, people lose their place in the task. Keep a mental bookmark: "I was on the Zip field."

## Pacing across a long session

Two or three hours of focused work is tiring, and the modules near the end count as much as the first ones.

- Use the gaps between modules to sit back, breathe and roll your shoulders.
- Keep water nearby.
- If your invitation allows breaks between sections, take a short one in the middle. Check the instructions before you assume you can pause.
- If you get kicked out by a technical problem, the help center says reopening your test link should take you back to where you left off. ([source](https://applicant.testgenius.com/knowledgebase/kicked-out-of-the-test))

## Practice against the clock

Untimed practice teaches the rules. Timed practice teaches the pace. Once your accuracy is good in the section guides, switch to timed runs such as the [free CritiCall practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/criticall?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=criticall), and keep a note of the item types that make you run long.

# FAQ

These are the questions people actually search for about CritiCall and 911 dispatcher testing, taken from Google's "People also ask" results in the US in November 2026, plus a few that come up in every applicant's inbox. Answers about the test come from the publisher or agency pages, linked in each answer.

## What is the CritiCall test?

A computer-based pre-employment test for 911 dispatchers, call takers and telecommunicators, made by Biddle Consulting Group as part of its TestGenius platform. It simulates a generic dispatch environment to see whether you have the skills the job needs, such as multitasking, data entry, memory and map reading. You do not need any dispatch knowledge. See the [test overview](test-overview.md).

## How long is the CritiCall exam?

It depends on how many modules your agency chooses. The publisher's [applicant FAQ](https://criticall911.com/company/news/criticall-dispatcher-applicant) says one to three hours on average, and the [TestGenius help center](https://applicant.testgenius.com/knowledgebase/how-long-does-the-test-take) recommends setting aside two to three hours so you can finish in one sitting.

## How hard is it to pass the CritiCall test?

It depends on the modules and on your agency's standard, which is not published by the publisher. Most people find the multitasking parts hardest, because a decision message can pop up while you are typing. The good news is that these are learnable skills. Knowing the four decision rules cold, chunking numbers, and practicing typing make a large difference within a week. Start with the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md).

## How hard is the police dispatcher test?

For police and sheriff's dispatch jobs, the skills test is often CritiCall, and the difficulty is the same as above: speed and multitasking rather than hard knowledge. The harder parts of police dispatch hiring are often what comes after the test. Agencies such as [Prince William County](https://www.pwcva.gov/department/public-safety-communications/hiring-process) and [Arizona DPS](https://azdps.gov/sites/default/files/2025-04/PCD_Exam_Plan_0425.pdf) list a polygraph, a background investigation and other checks after testing.

## How do I know if I passed the CritiCall test?

The agency tells you. The [TestGenius help center](https://applicant.testgenius.com/knowledgebase/where-can-i-get-my-test-results) says scores belong to the agency, and most agencies do not give out actual results. They let you know whether you are advancing, going onto an eligibility list, or did not pass. If you have not heard, contact the agency's hiring team, not TestGenius.

## What is a passing score on CritiCall?

There is no single passing score. Each agency sets its own. The City of Colorado Springs, for example, [says](https://coloradosprings.gov/node/101016) applicants need a score of 75 or better. Arizona DPS [ranks](https://azdps.gov/sites/default/files/2025-04/PCD_Exam_Plan_0425.pdf) passing candidates on an eligibility list. See [how scoring works](scoring.md).

## What WPM does a 911 dispatcher need?

There is no CritiCall-wide number. The keyboarding module is scored in words per minute, but each agency decides what speed it requires, and some run their own typing test as well. Your job posting is the place to look. If it gives no number, practice until you can type names, addresses and numbers steadily with almost no errors. See the [data entry guide](sections/data-entry-and-keyboarding.md#timing).

## How to practice for the dispatcher test?

- Read the official [candidate preparation guide](https://criticall911.com/hubfs/Criticall-Website-Files/CritiCall_Candidate_Test_Preparation_Guide_2023.pdf) and learn its four decision rules.
- Work through the [section guides](../README.md#contents) here, one module at a time.
- Practice typing names, addresses and phone numbers that someone reads to you.
- Run the free TestGenius sample tests linked on the [CritiCall applicant page](https://criticall911.com/dispatcher-testing/applicants) to check your device.
- Do a timed run, such as the [free CritiCall practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/criticall?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=criticall).

The [7-day plan](study-plans.md#7-day-plan) puts this in order.

## Is there an official CritiCall practice test?

No. The publisher's [applicant FAQ](https://criticall911.com/company/news/criticall-dispatcher-applicant) says there is no practice test and the software is only available to agencies and verified private dispatch companies. It does offer free sample tests for keyboarding, proofreading and spelling. The help center says these are not scored and are meant to check that your device works.

## Is CritiCall the same at every agency?

No. The publisher says CritiCall has more than 20 tests and each agency selects the ones that suit its positions. Two agencies can give quite different tests. If your invitation or posting lists modules, focus there.

## What is CritiCall3D?

The newest version. The publisher describes three parts: Operational (the skills tests, including a OneTest that combines eight classic tests), Situational (a situational judgment test for dispatch, with an emotional intelligence component) and Behavioral (a personality test). See the [situational judgment guide](sections/situational-judgment-and-personality.md).

## Can I take CritiCall on my phone?

No. The [help center](https://applicant.testgenius.com/knowledgebase/what-device-should-i-use-for-testing) says to use a laptop or desktop with Windows 10 or later or macOS 15 or later, and that some modules do not work on phones or tablets.

## Can I retake CritiCall?

That depends on the agency. Frederick County, Maryland, for example, [says](https://frederickcountymd.gov/8368/Hiring-Process) candidates who do not reach the required score are welcome to apply again. The publisher does not publish a retake rule, and TestGenius support cannot reopen tests. Ask the agency.

## How to interview for a 911 dispatcher position?

Formats vary. Prince William County uses a panel interview. Colorado Springs uses an online video interview: its [page](https://coloradosprings.gov/node/101016) says there are 10 questions, with 1 minute to prepare and 3 minutes to answer each, to be done within 5 days of receiving it. Prepare short, specific stories about staying calm under pressure, handling an upset person, following rules exactly, and juggling several tasks. Those are the same qualities CritiCall tests.

## What disqualifies you from becoming a 911 dispatcher?

Each agency sets its own standards, usually checked during the background stage, not on CritiCall. As one example, [Prince William County](https://www.pwcva.gov/department/public-safety-communications/hiring-process) lists disqualifiers including felony convictions, illegal drug use within three years before applying, a DUI-related conviction within two years, and giving false or misleading information during the process. Colorado Springs [says](https://coloradosprings.gov/node/101016) marijuana or CBD use within three months of the initial testing date makes applicants ineligible. Read your agency's own list before you apply.

## How hard is it to get hired as a dispatcher?

The process is long and has many steps. Prince William County says its process takes about two to three months after you apply, and it lists more than ten phases. Many applicants drop out along the way. Passing CritiCall well is the first step, and the one you can prepare for most directly.

## Why do 911 dispatchers quit?

The publisher's [applicant FAQ](https://criticall911.com/company/news/criticall-dispatcher-applicant) says the stressful environment and multitasking demands lead some new dispatchers to quit in their first week on the job, even after a costly training academy. That is one reason agencies use a realistic test like CritiCall: it helps both sides see whether the job is a good fit before training starts.

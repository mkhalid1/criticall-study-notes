# For 911 call taker and dispatcher applicants

Most people who take CritiCall are applying for a job as a 911 call taker, dispatcher or public-safety telecommunicator. This page explains where the test sits in a typical hiring process, what that means for your preparation, and what the job asks of you that the test is trying to see.

## Where it sits in a typical process

CritiCall usually comes early, right after the application. Agencies use it as a skills screen before spending time on interviews and background checks. A few real examples, each from the agency's own page:

| Agency | What it says |
|---|---|
| [Prince William County, Virginia](https://www.pwcva.gov/department/public-safety-communications/hiring-process) | Online application, then CritiCall testing, then a panel interview, personal history statement, conditional offer, pre-screen interview, polygraph, background interview and investigation, medical and physical exams, psychological exam, an interview with the Director, and final approval. The whole process takes about two to three months. |
| [Frederick County, Maryland](https://frederickcountymd.gov/8368/Hiring-Process) | One-stop hiring events that include the application, CritiCall skills testing, an interview and orientation. Successful candidates get a conditional offer, then a background investigation, psychological evaluation and physical and drug screen. |
| [City of Colorado Springs](https://coloradosprings.gov/node/101016) | Application, then a possible typing test and CritiCall (a score of 75 or better), then an online video interview, a contingent offer, a background investigation of about six weeks with polygraph and psychological evaluation, and a selection committee review. |
| [Arizona Department of Public Safety](https://azdps.gov/sites/default/files/2025-04/PCD_Exam_Plan_0425.pdf) | CritiCall is phase one and is weighted at 100%. Candidates who pass are ranked on an eligibility list, then invited to an interview, polygraph, drug test, background investigation, and hearing and vision tests. |
| [Grand Traverse County, Michigan](https://gtcountymi.gov/438/Emergency-Telecommunicator-Testing) | Selected candidates take CritiCall, with modules including data entry, memory, multitasking, map reading and note taking. A passing score is required before further consideration. |

The pattern: application, CritiCall, interview, then a long background stage. Your agency's order may differ, so read its posting.

## What this means for your preparation

- **CritiCall is often the first gate.** If you do not pass it, nothing else in your application gets seen. That makes it worth a week of preparation.
- **A ranked list rewards every point.** Where agencies rank candidates, like Arizona DPS, a higher score can mean an earlier interview, not just a pass.
- **Your invitation comes by email.** The help center says to look for an invitation from onlinetesting@invitations.testgenius.com or info@governmentjobs.com, which contains your personal test link. Check your spam folder. ([source](https://applicant.testgenius.com/knowledgebase/where-do-i-test))
- **The agency, not TestGenius, controls your test.** Extensions, reopened links and results all go through the agency. ([source](https://applicant.testgenius.com/knowledgebase/can-i-get-an-extension-for-my-test))
- **Some agencies test remotely with proctoring.** If yours does, the help center lists the requirements: a laptop or desktop, Windows 10 or 11 or macOS 15 or later, the latest Chrome, at least 8 GB of memory, at least 10 Mbps upload and download, a working webcam and microphone, and only one monitor. ([source](https://applicant.testgenius.com/knowledgebase/what-are-the-system-requirements-for-the-proctored-exam))

## Why the job needs these skills

The publisher calls CritiCall a realistic job preview, and its applicant FAQ notes that some people realize during the test that dispatch is not for them. That is not a bad outcome. The job really does involve:

- **Listening and typing at the same time,** while the caller is upset and the details come out of order.
- **Following protocols exactly,** even when your instinct says otherwise. That is why the decision rules on the test must be applied as written.
- **Several things at once.** A radio call can come in while you are on a 911 call. Multitasking is the skill the publisher puts first.
- **Calm under pressure.** The CritiCall3D Behavioral part measures Resilience/Composure for this reason.

If you find the multitasking modules stressful at first, that is normal. They get much easier with a few days of practice, because most of the stress comes from not yet having a routine.

## Habits that trip up dispatcher applicants

- **Using real-world knowledge on decision items.** People with medical, fire or security backgrounds often answer from experience. On the test, the four decision rules win.
- **Testing on the wrong device.** A work laptop with locked-down settings, or a tablet, can fail mid-test. Use a laptop or desktop you control, and run a free sample test first.
- **Not using a headset.** Audio items are much harder through laptop speakers in a noisy room.
- **Treating typing as an afterthought.** It feeds data entry, call summarization, memory and keyboarding.

## Practice before you apply

If you are thinking about applying, practice before the application, not after. The invitation can arrive quickly. Work through the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md) and the [7-day plan](study-plans.md#7-day-plan), and do a timed run of the [free CritiCall practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/criticall?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=criticall).

For career information, the publisher points applicants to the industry associations: APCO International, the International Academies of Emergency Dispatch, NENA and NECI. Links are on the [sources page](sources.md#industry-associations).

# CritiCall Study Notes

A free, complete study guide for CritiCall, the pre-employment test that many 911 centers use to screen dispatcher, call taker and telecommunicator applicants. It covers how the test works, a clear method for every module group, 50 original practice items with full explanations, two original practice maps, how agencies use the scores, and study plans for a week or a weekend. Written for people applying to 911 and public-safety dispatch jobs, with a page on where CritiCall sits in a real [hiring process](docs/dispatcher-applicants.md). Facts about the test come only from the publisher, Biddle Consulting Group, and from agencies' own pages, each one [cited](docs/sources.md).

Want to see where you stand first? Take the [free timed CritiCall practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/criticall?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=criticall), or the shorter [5-minute diagnostic](docs/diagnostic.md) in this repo.

## Start here

1. **Find your weak modules.** Take the [5-minute diagnostic](docs/diagnostic.md). It has one item for each module group and tells you where to start.
2. **Fix the weakest module first.** Open its guide below, learn the method, then do its practice items. If your invitation lists the modules you will take, start with those.
3. **Follow a plan.** Use the [7-day plan](docs/study-plans.md#7-day-plan), or the [2-day plan](docs/study-plans.md#2-day-plan) if your test link has already arrived.

## Quick facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Publisher | Biddle Consulting Group, Inc., on its TestGenius platform |
| What it is | A computer-based work-sample test that simulates a generic dispatch environment |
| Modules | More than 20 tests in the library. Each agency chooses which ones you take. |
| Length | One to three hours on average, depending on your modules. The publisher's help center suggests setting aside two to three hours. |
| Versions | CritiCall Classic, and CritiCall3D (Operational, Situational and Behavioral parts) |
| Delivery | Online, self-scoring, often taken remotely. Agencies may add remote proctoring. |
| Device | Laptop or desktop. Some modules do not work on phones or tablets. |
| Experience needed | None. No dispatch knowledge or training is required. |
| Pass mark | None published by the publisher. Each agency sets its own standard. |
| Your score | Owned by the agency. Most agencies only tell you whether you are moving forward. |

Sources: [CritiCall](https://criticall911.com/dispatcher-testing/agencieshr), the [applicant FAQ](https://criticall911.com/company/news/criticall-dispatcher-applicant) and the [TestGenius help center](https://applicant.testgenius.com/knowledgebase), checked 24 November 2026.

## The modules at a glance

| Module group | You will | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| Decision making and prioritization | Send Police, Fire, EMS or Utility within 15 seconds, often mid-task | [Decisions](docs/sections/decision-making-and-prioritization.md) |
| Data entry and keyboarding | Type what you read or hear into the right field, fast and exactly | [Data entry](docs/sections/data-entry-and-keyboarding.md) |
| Call summarization | Listen to a call and capture the key facts | [Call summarization](docs/sections/call-summarization.md) |
| Memory recall | Remember pairs, codes and phone numbers for a few seconds | [Memory](docs/sections/memory-recall.md) |
| Cross-referencing and perceptual ability | Look things up in lists and compare strings | [Cross-referencing](docs/sections/cross-referencing-and-perceptual-ability.md) |
| Map reading and position locating | Pick the most direct legal route and locate callers | [Maps](docs/sections/map-reading-and-position-locating.md) |
| Probability and numerical ability | Choose the most likely fact and do job-style arithmetic | [Numbers](docs/sections/probability-and-numerical-ability.md) |
| Reading comprehension and sentence clarity | Read short passages and pick the clearest wording | [Reading](docs/sections/reading-comprehension-and-sentence-clarity.md) |
| Spelling and proofreading | Spell confusable words and fix errors | [Spelling](docs/sections/spelling-and-proofreading.md) |
| Situational judgment and personality | Handle tough calls well, and answer a work-style questionnaire | [Situational](docs/sections/situational-judgment-and-personality.md) |

Every module guide has the same parts: what it measures, how the questions look, a step-by-step method, the traps that cost points, timing advice, 5 original practice items with explanations, and a score table.

## How to use this repo

- **Read on GitHub.** Every page links to the previous page, the next page and back to these contents, so you can read it start to finish like a book.
- **Test yourself honestly.** Answers are hidden under "Answers and explanations". Commit to an answer before you open them.
- **Treat stimulus blocks like the real thing.** Call transcripts and memory sets sit in collapsible blocks. Open, read or study once for the time given, close, then answer.
- **Read every explanation,** including for items you got right. Most say why the nearest wrong answer is wrong, which is where the learning is.
- **Keep a miss log.** For each miss, write the habit you broke. The [study plans](docs/study-plans.md#how-to-review-a-missed-item) show how.
- **Practice timed.** The notes teach the methods. A timed run, such as the [free one on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/criticall?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=criticall), teaches the pace.
- **Clone or fork it** if you want to annotate your own copy. The structure also works as a GitBook space: see [SUMMARY.md](SUMMARY.md).

## About the practice items

All 50 module practice items, the 10 diagnostic items, the call transcripts and the two maps are original, written for these notes. None are taken from CritiCall, from Biddle Consulting Group's materials or from any prep site. They follow the published descriptions of each module so that the methods you learn here carry over to the real test. Real 911 centers follow their own protocols, so the prioritization and situational answers use common principles, not any one agency's rules.
